⚡ 3-Phase AC Motor Analysis — VFD vs Variac Control

🔷 Motor 1 — VFD Frequency Control

Variable Frequency Drive: V/f ratio kept constant → Power maintained

60 Hz

📐 Live Calculations

V = V/f Ratio = 4.00 V/Hz × f = 240 V
XL = 2π × f × L = 2π × 60 × 0.150 = 56.55 Ω
Z = √(R² + XL²) = √( 2.0² + 56.55² ) = 56.56 Ω
I = V / Z = 240 / 56.56 = 4.24 A
P = √3 × VLL × I × pf = 1.732 × 240 × 4.24 × 0.85 = 1480 W
N = 120 × f / P = 120 × 60 / 4 = 1740 RPM (actual)
Torque = P / ω = 1480 / 182.2 = 8.12 N·m

💡 VFD Control: The VFD adjusts BOTH frequency AND voltage proportionally (constant V/f ratio). This keeps the magnetic flux constant, so the motor maintains its torque and power output at any speed.

🟠 Motor 2 — Variac Voltage Control

Variable Voltage: Only voltage changes → Power drops with speed

240 V

📐 Live Calculations

V = 240 V (via Variac) | f = 60 Hz (fixed)
XL = 2π × f × L = 2π × 60 × 0.150 = 56.55 Ω (fixed)
Z = √(R² + XL²) = √( 2.0² + 56.55² ) = 56.56 Ω (fixed)
I = V / Z = 240 / 56.56 = 4.24 A
P = √3 × VLL × I × pf = 1.732 × 240 × 4.24 × 0.85 = 1480 W
N = ~ 1740 RPM (slip increases as V drops)
Torque = ∝ V² = ( 240 / 240 )² × 8.12 = 8.12 N·m

⚠️ Variac Control: Only voltage changes while frequency stays at 60 Hz. Torque drops proportional to V², causing the motor to slow down AND lose significant power at lower voltages.
